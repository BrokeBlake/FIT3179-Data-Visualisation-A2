Urban Sprawl: A Symptom of Modern Melbourne

Urban Sprawl refers to the widespread, most often unplanned expansion of residential areas at the edges of cities. Melbourne, dubbed the “Doughnut City” in the late 1980s, has witnessed a rapid outward growth over the last half century, reshaping the way people live, work, and move.

The impact of urban sprawl remains a highly debated topic among planners, economists, and communities.

Supporters highlight its role in making housing more affordable, providing families with larger homes and backyards, and aligning with consumer preferences towards low density living.

Critics, on the other hand, point to increased automobile dependency, inefficient land use, and the loss of prime agricultural land as major drawbacks.

Since the early 1990s, the Victorian Government has introduced several urban growth strategies designed to contain sprawl while promoting inner-city revitalisation. Use the graph above to explore the growth and decline of all of Melbourne's suburbs over the previous 50 years.

Most famously, the 2001 initiaitive Melbourne 2030 sought to curb urban sprawl by introducting urban growth boundaries , reducing the proportion of low-density housing, and redirecting development into designated "activity centres".

These policies have had a significant impact on revitalising Melbourne's innermost suburbs, as you can see in the above chart. CBD-adjacent suburbs such as Melbourne, Southbank, and Carlton have seen over 1000% growth in the last 50 years. The Docklands' population has increased from just 30 residents to over 40 thousand, more than a 100,000% increase.

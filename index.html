Urban Sprawl: A symptom of Modern Melbourne

Urban Sprawl refers to the widespread, most often unplanned expansion of residential areas at the edges of cities. Melbourne, dubbed the “Doughnut City” in the late 1980s, has witnessed a rapid outward growth over the last half century, reshaping the way people live, work, and move.

The impact of urban sprawl remains a highly debated topic among planners, economists, and communities.

Supporters highlight its role in making housing more affordable, providing families with larger homes and backyards, and aligning with consumer preferences towards low density living.
Critics, on the other hand, point to increased automobile dependency, inefficient land use, and the loss of prime agricultural land as major drawbacks.
